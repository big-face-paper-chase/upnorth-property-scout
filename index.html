How Michigan tax-foreclosure auctions work

  1. When property taxes go unpaid for about three years, the county treasurer (the "foreclosing governmental unit") forecloses and takes title.
  2. The county must offer the property at public auction. Most Michigan counties run theirs online at tax-sale.info (operated by Title Check, LLC).
  3. First auction: minimum bid ≈ back taxes + fees. Unsold parcels go to a minimum-bid re-offer, then a no-reserve auction where bidding can start near zero.
  4. Prior liens are generally wiped by court order — but do your own title research. Always verify with the county treasurer before bidding.

What this scout does

Every 30 minutes it checks for newly posted auction catalogs, watches live auction days for lots selling in real time, and runs a full re-scan daily — downloading the official parcel spreadsheets and tracking new listings, price changes, and sales. It flags waterfront lots, cheap houses, big cheap acreage, and under-$1,000 lots automatically.

Before you bid

  • Properties are sold as-is. Interiors are usually unknown; many are occupied — be respectful.
  • Bidding requires registration + a deposit (typically a $1,000 card pre-authorization).
  • Check zoning, access, and back-utility bills with the local unit. This site is a research aid, not advice.
